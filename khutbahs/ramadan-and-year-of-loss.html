Khutbah: Ramadan and the Year of Loss

Target Audience: Ramadan Day 9 / Dealing with Grief & Hardship

السلام عليكم ورحمة الله وبركاته

My dear brothers and sisters, today is the 9th of Ramadan, and as we move through this blessed month, I want to remind you of a powerful and real moment from the life of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ—a moment that teaches us about pain, patience, and trust in Allah, even in Ramadan.

Around this time in Ramadan, the Prophet ﷺ lost one of the most important people in his life: Khadijah bint Khuwaylid رত্তির الله عنها—his wife, his supporter, and the first person to believe in his message.

Khadijah رضي الله عنها was not just the Prophet's wife. She was the one who believed in him when no one else did. When people called him a liar, she said, "Allah will never disgrace you." (narrated in Sahih al-Bukhari) When he was scared after the first revelation, she comforted him. When Islam was mocked and attacked, she stood firm beside him.

Her death happened during a year so painful that it became known as 'Aam al-Huzn'—the Year of Sorrow. And this loss came even though the Prophet ﷺ was one of the most beloved people to Allah.

This teaches us something very important: being close to Allah does not mean life will be easy. Even in Ramadan. Even for the best of people. Pain, loss, and hardship are part of this life—but Allah never leaves those who trust Him.

Despite his grief, the Prophet ﷺ did not turn away from Allah. He did not give up. He continued to pray, to call people to Islam, and to trust that Allah's plan was greater than his pain.

As youth, many of us go through our own struggles—stress, loss, loneliness, family problems, or silent pain that no one sees. Ramadan reminds us that it is okay to hurt, but it is not okay to lose hope in Allah. On this 9th day of Ramadan, remember that Allah sees every tear, hears every dua, and knows every struggle.

أقيموا الصلاة
