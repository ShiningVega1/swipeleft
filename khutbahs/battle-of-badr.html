Khutbah: Battle of Badr

Target Audience: Mid-Ramadan / Islamic History & Trust

السلام عليكم ورحمة الله وبركاته

My dear brothers and sisters, today is the 16th of Ramadan, and as we approach the middle of this blessed month, I want to remind you of one of the most important events in Islamic history - the Battle of Badr, which took place in Ramadan in the second year after Hijrah.

The Muslims who set out that day were only around 313 people. They were not trained soldiers. Many of them had little to no armor, and some did not even have proper weapons. On the other side were the Quraysh of Makkah—nearly 1,000 men, fully armed and confident.

The Muslims did not leave Madinah intending to fight a full army. But when the situation changed, they stood firm for the sake of Allah.

On the night before the battle, the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ spent the entire night in du'a. He prayed so intensely that his cloak fell from his shoulders. He begged Allah for victory, showing complete reliance on Him.

Allah answered that du'a. On the 17th of Ramadan, Allah granted the Muslims victory in a battle that seemed impossible. The Qur'an calls this day Yawm al-Furqan—the Day that separated truth from falsehood.

This battle teaches us that numbers, strength, and status do not guarantee success. What matters is iman, sincerity, and trust in Allah.

As youth today, we may feel outnumbered by distractions, temptations, and pressure to fit in. The Battle of Badr reminds us that standing with truth, even when you feel small or alone, is never a loss. On this 16th day of Ramadan, let us remember that victory begins before the battle—in our du'a, our sincerity, and our connection with Allah.
