Etiquettes of Practicing in the Summer - Practicality of Haya

Target Audience: Seasonal / High School / College Youth

إن الحمد لله، نحمده ونستعينه ونستغفره، ونعوذ بالله من شرور أنفسنا ومن سيِّئات أعمالنا من يهده الله فلا مُضلَّ له، ومن يضلل فلا هادي له. وأشهد أن لا إله إلا الله وحده لا شريك له، وأشهد أن محمدا عبده ورسوله، أما بعد.

Brothers, summer is upon us. The days are long, the sun is bright, and our schedules are full. Families are heading to parks, beaches, and pools. We are almost out of school. Barbecues are lit, road trips are planned, and the energy of the season is real. There is nothing wrong with any of this. Islam did not come to strip us of joy. It came to guide us within our joy.

But in the middle of all of that activity, something quietly slips away for many of us. The prayer. Dhuhr passes while we are at the park. Asr goes by while we are still at the beach. And we tell ourselves: "I'll pray when I get home." Then we get home, and the moment has passed, or we pray it so late that it barely counts. This is a pattern that too many of us fall into every summer, and today we need to speak honestly about it.

We also need to address something that comes up every summer in our community, quietly but consistently: what is the proper covering for a Muslim man when he is swimming, at a cookout, or spending time outdoors? These are practical, real questions, and Islamic scholarship has clear answers. Let us address them firmly so we can guard our reward.
